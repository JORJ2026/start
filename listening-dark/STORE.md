# Storefront copy

**The Listening Dark**
*You cannot see. You can only call, and listen to what answers.*

**Short description (under 160 characters)**
A stealth game played by sound. Call into a drowned cave, read the echoes, bring the lost home, and slip past the things that hear you too.

**Long description**

The colony stopped singing the night the water rose. You are Quill, the last of them who still calls into the dark.

In *The Listening Dark*, the screen is black until you make a sound. Each call sends out a wavefront, and the cave appears as its echoes return: thousands of pale points along wet stone that fade almost as soon as you see them. A quiet chirp shows you the next few metres. A long call lights the whole cavern, and tells everything in it exactly where you are.

Down here, seeing has a price.

- **A story told in the dark.** Eight chapters, an elder with a secret, six lost voices to find by sound alone, and something enormous at the bottom that used to sing. Three endings.
- **Hear before you see.** Hunters click and growl in full stereo. Water drips light the rock in tiny flashes. The way down hums, and you can follow it by ear.
- **Echoes show the past.** A hunter appears where your call caught it, not where it is now.
- **Every call is a choice.** Fast wings are loud; gliding is silent. Bellflowers ring when called, and make perfect decoys.
- **Roguelite modes** for after the story: Descent, a Daily cave, and the Endless deep, with twelve boons and seventeen achievements.
- **Plays your way.** Keyboard and mouse, controller (with vibration and the right button prompts), or touch. Full settings, on-screen sound cues, mono audio, adjustable text size.

Everything you see and hear is generated as you play: no textures and no samples. Just a cave, a voice, and the dark.

**Tags:** stealth, atmospheric, story rich, roguelite, procedural, audio-driven, minimalist, controller support, touch

**Platform:** browser (desktop and mobile), single HTML file, works offline.
**Play time:** the story should take roughly one to two hours (an estimate; not timed with players). Daily and Endless are open-ended.
